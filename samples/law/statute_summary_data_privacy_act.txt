

CASCADIA CONSUMER DATA PRIVACY ACT (CCDPA)
Statute Summary — Cas. Rev. Stat. §§ 19.400–19.475 [fictitious]
Effective Date: January 1, 2025

SECTION 1 — PURPOSE AND SCOPE
The CCDPA establishes consumer rights over personal data collected by businesses
operating in the State of Cascadia. It applies to any entity that (a) controls or
processes personal data of 25,000 or more Cascadia residents annually, or (b)
derives more than 40% of gross revenue from the sale of personal data.

SECTION 2 — CONSUMER RIGHTS (§ 19.410)
Consumers have the right to:
  (a) Access — obtain confirmation of whether their personal data is processed
      and receive a copy of that data within 30 days of a verified request.
  (b) Deletion — request deletion of personal data, subject to statutory
      exceptions (e.g., data required for legal compliance or fraud prevention).
  (c) Correction — request correction of inaccurate personal data.
  (d) Opt-Out — opt out of the sale of personal data and of targeted advertising.
  (e) Portability — receive personal data in a portable, machine-readable format.

SECTION 3 — BUSINESS OBLIGATIONS (§ 19.425)
  3.1 Businesses must provide a clear privacy notice describing categories of
      data collected, purposes of processing, and third parties with whom data
      is shared.
  3.2 Businesses must implement "reasonable administrative, technical, and
      physical safeguards" appropriate to the volume and sensitivity of data
      processed.
  3.3 Businesses must honor consumer requests under Section 2 within 45 days,
      extendable once by 45 additional days with notice to the consumer.
  3.4 Businesses may not discriminate against consumers for exercising rights
      under this Act (e.g., denying service or charging a different price).

SECTION 4 — SENSITIVE DATA (§ 19.440)
Processing of "sensitive data" — including precise geolocation, biometric data,
health data, and data revealing racial or ethnic origin — requires opt-in
consent rather than opt-out.

SECTION 5 — ENFORCEMENT (§ 19.460)
  5.1 The Cascadia Attorney General may bring enforcement actions with civil
      penalties of up to $7,500 per violation.
  5.2 A 30-day cure period applies for first-time violations, after which
      penalties may be assessed without further notice.
  5.3 The Act does not create a private right of action, except for violations
      arising from a data breach caused by failure to implement reasonable
      security safeguards under Section 3.2, per § 19.462.

SECTION 6 — EXEMPTIONS (§ 19.470)
Exempt entities include: nonprofit organizations, government agencies, and data
already regulated under the Cascadia Health Records Confidentiality Act, to the
extent of that overlap.
