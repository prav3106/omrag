
CASE BRIEF

Case: Alvarez v. Northgate Logistics Corp.
Citation: 214 Cas.App.3d 552 (Cascadia Court of Appeals, 2023) [fictitious]
Court: Cascadia Court of Appeals, Third Division

FACTS
Maria Alvarez was employed as a warehouse coordinator at a Northgate Logistics
distribution center. In June 2022, Alvarez reported to her supervisor that a
forklift used on the night shift had a malfunctioning brake sensor. Two weeks
later, Alvarez was terminated for "restructuring," despite the company having
hired a replacement coordinator within the same month. Alvarez filed suit alleging
retaliatory termination in violation of the Cascadia Whistleblower Protection Act
("CWPA"), Cas. Rev. Stat. § 49.860.

PROCEDURAL HISTORY
The trial court granted summary judgment for Northgate Logistics, holding that
Alvarez failed to establish a causal connection between her safety report and
her termination. Alvarez appealed.

ISSUE
Whether temporal proximity between a protected safety disclosure and an adverse
employment action, combined with evidence of a pretextual reason for termination,
is sufficient to survive summary judgment under CWPA § 49.860.

HOLDING
Reversed and remanded. The Court of Appeals held that a two-week gap between a
protected disclosure and termination, paired with evidence that the stated reason
("restructuring") was contradicted by the immediate hiring of a replacement,
creates a genuine issue of material fact regarding retaliatory motive.

REASONING
The court applied the three-part burden-shifting framework: (1) Alvarez engaged
in protected activity by reporting a safety hazard; (2) Northgate took an adverse
action; and (3) a reasonable jury could infer causation from timing and pretext
evidence. The court emphasized that "close temporal proximity, standing alone,
may be sufficient at the prima facie stage, but pretext evidence materially
strengthens the inference of retaliatory intent." The court distinguished this
case from Doremus v. Cascadia Foods, 198 Cas.App.2d 41 (2019) [fictitious], where
a six-month gap and a documented pattern of poor performance reviews defeated
the retaliation claim.

DISPOSITION
Summary judgment reversed. Case remanded to the trial court for further
proceedings on the merits of the retaliation claim.

KEY TAKEAWAY
Employers relying on a "restructuring" defense should expect close scrutiny when
a replacement hire follows shortly after a termination that closely trails a
protected disclosure.
